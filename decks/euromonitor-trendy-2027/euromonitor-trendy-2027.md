## Слайд 5 — Shelf Assured

Products disappearing from shelves, reformulations without warning and prices rising unpredictably have become regular occurrences.

Consumers now actively manage their exposure to these disruptions, scrutinising what they buy and demanding complete reliability from their choices.

Value is evolving into confidence that the products and services people rely on today won’t let them down tomorrow, in quality, cost or availability. Brands should make dependability a core part of value propositions.

### From best deal to safe bet

Cost pressures, supply shocks and service failures have stopped feeling temporary. Instability is now part of daily life.

72% of consumers worry about rising prices of everyday items, a concern that deepens with age.¹ And more than half only buy from brands they trust¹—trust made fragile by stock shortages, reformulations, shrinking packs and tech outages. The result: availability anxiety.

In 2027, the meaning of value expands into a broader question: can I count on this to still be available, work the same and cost what I expect next month, or even years from now?

## Слайд 12 — Shelf Assured: Regional implications

North America North America is one region where businesses are moving faster than shoppers.

Consumer caution is relatively subdued; around half only buy from trusted brands and research products in detail.⁶ But businesses aren’t taking any chances, with professionals reporting high rates of supply diversification and risk monitoring (42% and 36%, respectively) at their organisations.⁷

Building on COVID-19-era lessons, companies that restructured supply chains then are better equipped to navigate Trump’s tariffs now—from absorbing costs to securing continuity of supply at the right price through diversified suppliers.

North American businesses still face struggling demand, wavering consumer trust and affordability concerns. And engineered reliance raises the stakes—a reminder that the deeper you embed a brand into someone's routine, the less room for error.

Latin America Latin America is where consumers really can’t afford disruption. These consumers are among the most anxious about rising prices (73%)⁸ while also having limited buffers; savings are projected at 9% of disposable income in 2027, well below the global forecast of 15%.⁹

Businesses are responding. 63% of professionals said their company cut costs through operational efficiency, and 55% have already renegotiated supplier or partner contracts, the strongest response of any region. But only 20% enhanced supply chain risk monitoring, the lowest anywhere.¹⁰ Infrastructure explains part of the gap: gross fixed capital formation is expected to account for just 18% of GDP in 2027, down from 20% in 2022,¹¹ and logistics remain fragmented.

Companies can’t diversify supply fast enough to match consumer needs, so a single stockout doesn't just lose a sale but shifts a shopper to a competing brand that proves more reliable.

Middle East and Africa The Middle East and Africa region sits at the intersection of global trade disruption. The Iran war and the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz made supply volatility a daily reality for these consumers. The region has a young, digitally connected population that demands reliability.

Businesses had to quickly diversify supply, turning continuity into a commercial race and making dependability a precondition for brand competitiveness.

## Слайд 14 — Shelf Assured: Trend applications M&S

M&S turned brand shrinkflation into a private label opportunity. In 2025, as certain brands downsized festive tins, M&S promoted a larger, own-label version—subtly positioning itself as the better-value alternative.

This shows the exclusion risk at the heart of the Shelf Assured trend: retailers actively steering shoppers towards their own equivalents when name brands fall short.

### Calbee

Calbee turned a potential disruption into a test of transparent communication. When Middle East tensions threatened printing ink supply in May 2026, the Japanese snack giant pre-emptively switched some products to temporary black-and-white packaging, keeping the actual formulations unchanged.

Sales surged initially, then dropped as the unfamiliar packaging created confusion in a category built on consistency.

### PlayStation

Sony is accelerating gaming’s shift to digital distribution and platform dependence, with disc production ending in 2028. But a global PlayStation Network outage in July 2026 left gamers unable to play for roughly eight hours, highlighting how access depends as much on the software and services behind the console as the hardware itself.

## Слайд 15 — Shelf Assured: Tactics for 2027

### Protect consistency alongside Audit your portfolio for fragility Build supply flexibility

Map consumer reliance against supply risk across your portfolio, then prioritise the essentials and favourites people return to repeatedly. For these products and services, a single stockout or outage could lose a customer for good.

This exercise focuses your investment on protecting the most revenue rather than spreading resources thin among products where disruption matters less.

Don't rely on a single supplier for your ingredients or materials. With multiple sources, you can absorb commodity shocks and keep trusted products available, consistent and affordable.

The result: stronger margins without eroding the customer relationship, turning supply chain investment into a loyalty advantage.

### availability

Availability alone doesn’t equal dependability.

Your products or services should also remain consistent, or at least deliver the experience and outcome expected. That includes maintaining support for software-dependent products and communicating clearly before changing interfaces or removing features.

## Слайд 16 — Reluctant Reliance

Consumers are drawing boundaries around AI, from its outputs to its societal impact.

People want tools that automate routine tasks, simplify complex choices and streamline decision-making. But they’re wary of privacy risks, misinformation, opaque systems, environmental costs and loss of agency.

As AI becomes ambient and harder to avoid, a new tension emerges where the tech is increasingly useful in daily life but still a source of friction.

For businesses, the signal is clear: adoption depends less on how much the tech can do and more on whether consumers feel confident and comfortable using AI. The winners will be those that make AI useful, transparent and easy to control, with distinct lines between assistance and autonomy.

## Слайд 23 — Reluctant Reliance: Regional implications

North America North America is where this trend is most commercially visible. Consumers use AI to simplify decisions, but broader trust remains fragile.

In the US, concern about AI’s societal impact is especially pronounced, creating a sharper divide between practical adoption and emotional comfort. Asia Pacific Scale, digital intensity and sharp market variation shape this trend in Asia Pacific.

## Слайд 59 — Future Tense: Regional implications

Western Europe Western Europe is where traditional assumptions ran longest and belief is now weakest. Just 44% of consumers think their investments will hold long-term value and 49% expect a more dangerous world within five years—the lowest and highest rates, respectively, of any region.⁴⁸

The response is subtle rather than radical. Consumers are shying away from irreversible commitments across housing and mobility, for example. Long-stay work-live formats such as Zoku and flexible-serviced apartments such as Bob W stand in for permanent addresses, while car subscriptions such as FINN and SIXT+ replace ownership and multi-year leases.

Mature welfare states under fiscal strain and stretched housing markets mean this region has the most future to lose confidence in. The buyer here is defensive but not yet distressed; proof and flexibility beat aspiration.

Eastern Europe Nowhere is the outlook more heavily discounted than Eastern Europe. Only a third of consumers expect to be better off financially in five years and a similar proportion expect life to get worse,²⁸ the weakest and gloomiest sentiments, respectively, across all regions. At the same time, half said they enjoy life without worrying about planning for the future.²⁸

Disengagement shaped by war in the region and lived memory of systems that failed previously are channelling spend into small, immediate, everyday comforts rather than long commitments.

That climate favours present-forward, impulse spending. Żabka Group has become emblematic of this shift, building its business around convenience and everyday purchases. The retailer recorded double-digit sales growth in 2025⁴⁹ and opened more than 1,300 stores across Poland and Romania that year.⁵⁰

Middle East and Africa Middle East and African consumers are the most optimistic anywhere. 66% expect to be better off financially in five years,⁴² which means that present-focus here often takes the form of celebration and self-investment. Whether this sentiment holds could depend in part on how the ongoing conflict in Iran unfolds.

Spiritual life supplies the long-term anchor that formal institutions provide elsewhere. 71% of consumers said spiritual beliefs are important, the highest of any region.⁴² Governments across the Gulf are investing in entertainment, tourism and live events; the sixth edition of Riyadh Season drew more than 11 million visitors to Saudi Arabia by late December 2025.

In this region, the potential to pitch the future as outright opportunity, rather than something to be hedged against, is greater than any other.

## Слайд 60 — Future Tense: Trend applications Mercado Pago

Mercado Libre, a major Latin American e-commerce business, built a broader financial services ecosystem through Mercado Pago, its digital payments platform. The cashback earned through Meli+ (its loyalty membership) lands in a Mercado Pago account and generates automatic daily interest, so spending and growing money stop being opposites.

### Dopamine Shop

Dopamine Shop is a free shopping simulator inspired by South Korea’s “dopamine sites” that gained traction in 2026.

These fake online stores offer a less costly means of retail therapy, allowing users to browse products, fill a cart and complete a checkout without ever spending money or receiving an order. The experience captures the excitement and anticipation of purchasing without the financial consequences of a real transaction.

### Gucci

Rather than abandoning luxury altogether, consumers are seeking more attainable ways to access premium brands and enjoy value in the present.

## Слайд 61 — Future Tense: Trend applications Polymarket

Polymarket is a prediction market platform where people trade on the likelihood of future events, such as elections, sports and economic outcomes.

Nearly a third of Gen Z and almost a quarter of millennials in the US said they’re currently or considering putting their money into prediction markets or sports betting.⁵¹ This growing interest reflects a shift towards treating speculative, fast-moving forms of investing as part of everyday money management.

### The People’s Union USA

The People’s Union USA has turned the one-day boycott into infrastructure, publishing a rolling calendar of economic blackouts and no-spend days through 2025 and 2026. Data measured little movement in national sales, but the shift from spontaneous outrage to scheduled, named, repeatable non-consumption is a coming challenge for brands.

This grassroots movement advocates for economic accountability and equality, reflecting a broader push towards greater opportunity for future generations.

### Astrotalk

Astrotalk is a digital astrology marketplace based in India that connects users in more than 60 countries with human astrologers, numerologists, tarot readers and other forms of guidance, alongside an e-commerce store of spiritual products. A 2026 employee share buyback propelled the company to unicorn status, reaching a USD1 billion valuation without new venture funding. Astrotalk reflects growing interest in alternative belief systems that offer meaning, reassurance and a sense of control amid an uncertain future.

## Слайд 62 — Future Tense: Tactics for 2027

### Lead with immediate value Commit to credibility Serve alternative timescales

Reposition product and service claims against outcomes a consumer will validate within one purchase cycle, and make the long-term benefit part of the overall proof package rather than the headline.

This improves conversion and helps justify pricing where longer-horizon claims are losing support among shoppers.

Audit your portfolio for underfunded and overleveraged claims about the future. Retire promises that can’t be substantiated, whether around sustainability, ethical practices or social impact, and be transparent about sourcing, pricing and trade-offs.

Where the brand’s identity genuinely supports a change-oriented stance, lean in and accept that in this climate some consumers will be lost.

Design products and rituals that anchor to rhythms and metrics beyond typical market dynamics like seasonal ranges, nature-based experiences or formats for reflection. Partner with the faith, mindfulness and outdoor platforms these consumers already trust.
